Also in the archive — the machines on the tape

Four systems produced the logs above. Each one is described the way it describes itself: with the hardest true number first.

W—01

Laya + Divergence Guard

A local decision server that classifies, ranks and judges — 11,192 decisions on its own log. It faithfully reproduces the calls its operator accepted, and it just as faithfully repeats the calls he rejected: same side, high confidence, weeks later. So he built a second system whose only job is to catch the first one doing it, and flag it.

W—04

Switchboard / Omnirouter

A local router for AI traffic with a receipt on every call: which model was asked, which model actually served, what fell back, what errored. The claim is not that every lane is healthy — on Oct 22 the receipts, not the health check, showed the leader lane capped at zero serves. The claim is that the receipts exist, and they don't flatter anyone.

Oct 22 — Falsification Day

One day, spent trying to prove his own systems wrong. The standard is the check that could kill it.

OCT 22

The feed. 62 postings labeled "verified live," re-checked against each employer's own open-jobs list: 22 confirmed live, 12 dead, 2 with wrong details, 27 unproven either way. The dead and the wrong were pulled the same day; the unproven were relabeled unverified.

OCT 22

The ledger. 194 recorded job applications checked against employer email: 166 proven by a confirmation in the inbox; 26 had already been rejected while the records still showed them as open. Corrected the same day the findings landed.

OCT 22

The router. "Up" turned out to mean the process was running. Of 104 real calls that day, the leader lane served zero. Health now has to be reported per lane, from the receipts.
