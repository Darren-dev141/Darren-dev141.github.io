Reality Drops

MSCHF for reality itself: not art about society — a small piece of a different world, made real enough to measure.

MSCHF's best work doesn't comment on a system — it intervenes in one: a leaderboard that turns private wealth into a public ranking; thousands of keys and one shared car as a live experiment in ownership. The invisible rules become tangible because, for a while, they're different rules, actually in force.

A Reality Drop pushes one step further: from interventions that reveal systems to interventions that discover how systems could change. A drop is a deliberately constructed alteration to ordinary life — real participants, real stakes, a defined run — instrumented well enough to learn whether the alternative deserves to exist permanently. The difference between a stunt and an invention: a provocation asks what if?; reality engineering answers here it is, and measures whether it works.

DROP 001

The 72-Hour Job Market

Recruit cooperating employers to run an alternative hiring process that gives applicants substantive decisions within 72 hours. Real jobs, real salaries, real hires. The question: are conventional hiring delays actually necessary? Track cost, fairness, quality, and retention against the ordinary process.

DROP 002

One Block From the Future

Partner with a neighborhood to operate a temporary alternative set of local services — responsive maintenance, community-managed resources, new shared infrastructure. Residents experience a real, opt-in version of a future normally confined to planning documents.

DROP 003

The Impossible Things Department

Collect claims that something cannot be done — for economic, technical, institutional, or conventional reasons. Select one. Construct the smallest genuine counterexample, and publish exactly which constraints were overcome and which survived.

The guardrail, and the north star

MSCHF's controversies are part of the curriculum: experiments that turn living beings into stakes are a line, not a lesson in boldness. A drop requires meaningful consent, protects bystanders, and takes no irreversible harm for spectacle. A successful drop leaves something behind — a reproducible method, a discovery, a functioning institution, or a changed expectation of what's possible.

North star: don't just show people a different world. Make a small piece of that world real enough that they can experience it — and rigorous enough that we can learn whether it deserves to exist permanently.

All three drops are proposals. Their value depends on real partners, real resources, and rigorous evaluation — which is why this plant is a seedling with its status printed on it.
