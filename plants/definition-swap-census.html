The Meaning Is in the Options

A definition-swap census of a production decision system — where does the meaning of a machine's decision actually live?

Maturity
🌳 Evergreen
Planted
Oct 15, 2026
Last tended
Oct 22, 2026
Status
Paper draft v0

Agent systems are built out of small typed judgments: a judge model answers under a schema — named options, yes/no questions, score fields — and each option carries a short definition. Everyone assumes the judge decides by the definitions. The census tested that assumption on a complete production log: all 629 usable decisions, each re-judged twice — once under its original schema, once with the definitions exchanged behind unchanged labels.

The judge followed the labels, not the meanings. On choice fields the semantic pick reversed on 85.8% of items — including 94.6% on the highest-stakes gate in the system. Unswapped controls agreed at 99.6–100%, so the flips aren't noise: they're the judge reading the swapped text and obeying the label anyway. Worse, confidence points the wrong way — flipped picks carried higher mean confidence (0.158) than picks that survived (0.024). And score fields fail silently in the other direction: their values barely move under the same swap, so a dashboard watching scores sees stability on items whose choices just reversed.

The repair is specific, and it's the hopeful part: re-encode the same judgment as a choice over neutral labels with the definitions attached to the labels, and flips on real traffic fall to 14.8%. Encode it as a numeric score instead, and flips stay at 96.3%. The protection lives in the choice primitive — not in numerals, and not in confidence.

Why it's evergreen

It survived the tests designed to kill it: reproduction controls at ~100%, identity controls at 99.6–100%, and a pre-registered method applied to the full log rather than a convenient sample. Its limits are printed in the draft: the motivating industry study's citation is still being pinned down, one schema family's numeric arm is partial, and cross-system evidence is a 20-item pilot. A paper draft (v0) exists; the venue plan is arXiv first, then a 2027 workshop. Unsubmitted is not unpublished-by-accident — it's labeled.
