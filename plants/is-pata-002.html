IS-PATA-002 — The Limits of Imagination

Can an intelligence discover a limitation in the very language it uses to describe what is possible?

Pilot reported

The label first, because it matters here: this plant is reported, not verified. The pilot was built and run by a sibling process and audited with a checker the same author wrote. Its numbers are planted exactly as reported — and its most valuable property is that it reports its own failure.

The experiment: 240 synthetic worlds of six types, five strategies compared, 1,200 trial records. An "exception-first" strategy — the pataphysical candidate — improved substantially over a fixed inherited model, but did not meaningfully beat plain exhaustive search, and its resource saving (about 17% fewer instrument reads) fell short of the 25% threshold registered in advance. Under its own criteria, the claim of a distinctive pataphysical advantage fails this test. That is a useful result, and it's reported as one.

The sharper finding is underneath: every alternative the system "discovered" was already present in its author's candidate vocabulary. It searched a larger box; it did not invent a new kind of box. In the worlds built around an inaccessible hidden variable, all 40 instances stayed unresolved — and in 8 of them, the method adopted an extra mathematical feature that improved its fit without explaining the hidden mechanism at all. A better explanation of familiar data is not the discovery of what's missing.

What happens next

The follow-up design gives an AI an incomplete scientific vocabulary and tests whether it can propose genuinely new measurements or interventions — not merely select from formulas prepared for it — against program synthesis, random exploration, and explicit uncertainty as rivals. No external credits were used and the $10 reserve stayed untouched; the full package — source, pre-registration, trials, audit — is held with the reporting seat, which is precisely why this plant is a seedling until an independent run reproduces it.
