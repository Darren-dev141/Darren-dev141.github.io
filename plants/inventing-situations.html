Inventing Situations

Don't just argue about a system. Arrange the circumstances where an alternative version of it briefly exists — and measure.

Program, 1 instance run

The program, stated plainly: find a neglected possibility at the intersection of things you know; design a specific arrangement that makes an otherwise inaccessible result testable; build the smallest honest demonstration — an executable artifact, not a proposal; then try to destroy your own discovery before anyone else has to. Ambition is permitted. Dishonesty is not: no fabricated outcomes, no borrowed accomplishments.

Its first executed instance is in Bed 2: the Buyback, which took a question nobody could answer from logs alone — what is a judge's confidence actually worth? — and made it answerable by pricing it. Its second is a question, the Incursion Problem. Its research arm, IS-PATA-002, has already produced the program's favorite kind of result: a clean, pre-registered failure that sharpened the next question.

The standard it holds itself to

  • Estrangement is not knowledge. Making the familiar look strange can produce wonder without producing a single new fact.
  • The outsider is still our invention. An artificial alien perspective carries our assumptions in costume; distance is a different perspective, not a superior one.
  • Novelty can become an addiction. A program that rewards the most bizarre intervention becomes a machine for manufacturing astonishment.
  • Understanding doesn't guarantee transformation. Institutions absorb critique. A revolutionary perspective might change nothing — and the work has to be able to find that out.

That standard is the gate every situation in this program has to pass, including this page: if a situation can't state what would prove it pointless, it doesn't get built.
