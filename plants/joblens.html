JobLens

Is this job posting still real? A verifier that answers from the employer's own site — or honestly says it can't.

Job boards are full of ghosts: postings that closed weeks ago, listings with the wrong city, the wrong pay, the wrong title — all still collecting applications. JobLens takes a listing back to the employer's own open-jobs list and records what it finds there: a working apply path, a posting that no longer exists, details that don't match — or an honest "couldn't verify," which is a result, not a failure to be smoothed over.

On Oct 22, 2026, the same hard standard was turned inward, on the operator's own "verified live" job feed. Of 62 postings carrying that label, only 22 could be hard-confirmed on the employer's own list. Twelve were dead. Two carried wrong details. Twenty-seven could not be proven either way — and were relabeled unverified the same day, because a feed that grades everyone else's honesty doesn't get an exemption.

Why it isn't evergreen

The standard is proven; the coverage isn't. A hard test exists only where an employer's own list can be checked, and the listings with no hard label available are exactly the population where a shortcut would be most tempted to guess. JobLens doesn't guess — but "doesn't guess, on the subset it can reach" is a budding claim, not an evergreen one.
