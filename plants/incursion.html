The Incursion Problem

A multiverse of competing world models — and the design problem of keeping it from crowning a Doom.

The frame is borrowed from Hickman's Secret Wars, with the physics swapped for epistemology. Imagine not parallel universes but competing world models — each a different understanding of reality, each making predictions, each trying to survive.

  • An incursion happens whenever two models make incompatible claims about reality — forcing an experimental confrontation in which either, both, or neither may survive.
  • A lifeboat preserves the most valuable discoveries and mechanisms from models that collapse — with limited seats, so preservation is a choice with a cost.
  • A battleworld is what emerges when fragments of incompatible models are recombined into systems that can imagine possibilities neither predecessor could.

The question posed, verbatim in spirit: what situation would make this process self-sustaining, scientifically rigorous, and capable of discoveries no individual model — or human — anticipated? The pieces already exist in this garden in miniature: the Buyback is a one-shot incursion with stake-priced survival; the Chamber is a confrontation rig; the census is what a model looks like after losing one.

The anti-Doom rule

The hard constraint is governance: the system must never become its own Doctor Doom — an authority that decides what reality is, instead of a mechanism through which reality keeps proving it wrong.

Working rule, planted with the question: the operator can kill any model, but cannot save one. Survival is decided only by confrontations scored against reality — recorded outcomes and pre-registered rules — never by a model's confidence (on this system's own tape, flipped picks carried the higher confidence), and never by the model that authored the test.

Why it's budding

Because it stopped being only a question. The census it called for has been run over the production tape: 3,520 pairwise encounters between the judges and encodings already on disk produced 1,464 incursions — incompatible picks on identical items, counted as a population for the first time. The lifeboat registry exists too: five seats, admission by printed mechanical rules, no saves. Aboard: gpt-oss-120b (Class I — reality-adjudicated), Qwen3.5-397B (Class I, probationary), and one surviving fragment — claim × attached-numeric, on the choice axis only (Class II — it survived definition rotation). Two seats are empty. And the incumbent judge is not aboard: on the only adjudicated tape there is, it failed the admission bar.

What the census did not close is larger than what it did. Only 23 of the 1,464 incursions (1.6%) could be settled against recorded outcomes, with 9 more control-class; 1,432 (97.8%) are undecidable — the census tape and the race tape share zero outcome-joined items, so for almost every contradiction there is nothing on record that can say which side reality kept. One seat is probationary because its tape was still growing while the census ran, and the census has not yet been re-run on a frozen final tape. One real result, honestly bounded — that is budding, not evergreen. The garden tends it at the stage it earned.
