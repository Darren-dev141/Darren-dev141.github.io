QD-Rec

A paper recommender where every recommendation has to argue for its place — in writing.

First run complete

Most recommenders hand you a ranked list and keep their reasons to themselves. QD-Rec works differently: it's a quality-diversity archive. Each paper that enters has to carry a plain-language hypothesis — why this paper, for this researcher, and what to do with it — and a judge scores the hypothesis before the paper earns its place. Entries can be kept, replaced by a better occupant of the same niche, or discarded. Nothing is dropped silently: replaced and discarded entries stay on file with their reasons.

The first run worked a NeurIPS 2025 corpus against one research profile (decision models and calibration, agent harnesses, world models, formal verification). It seeded an archive of 10, then ran 24 evaluations, Thompson-sampling which papers to judge next. Every chat and embedding call ran through the local Omnirouter — embeddings computed on the same machine, no paid API.

Why it isn't evergreen

One run is one run. The judge that scores the hypotheses is the same class of judge the census found wanting, and the archive hasn't yet been tested against the only verdict that matters — whether the researcher actually reads, uses, and cites what it kept. Budding: a real artifact with a real method, awaiting its second season.
