MFlow — Pixel Diffusion, Rethought

Mukhopadhyay, Mai, Shrivastava & Mahapatra — the most useful result in the paper is the one its headline metric cannot see.

Reading
Full text, 33 pp.

The work: can a plain diffusion transformer generate images directly in pixel space, without a latent encoder? The paper rethinks two design choices — what the model predicts (a learned blend of clean-image and velocity prediction), and a small convolutional refiner trained afterward, on a frozen backbone, to repair a measured high-frequency deficit: pixel models were losing fine detail, and the standard score didn't show it.

The number that earns the plant: measured spectrally, the deficit shrinks from −6.63 dB to −1.29 dB after the refiner — while FID, the field's headline metric, sits at 1.81 before and 1.81 after. The fix is real and the scoreboard is blind to it. The honest edges are in the note too: at the largest scale MFlow's FID (1.81) actually trails a rival's (1.79); its clean win is video (FVD 122 against a latent model's 169); the refiner's parameter count is never stated; and no code repository exists — pseudocode and a project page only.

Why it grows here: not for image generation. For the pattern — a defect you can only fix after you build the instrument that can see it, and a small post-hoc patch on a frozen system instead of a retrain. This garden's own instruments keep relearning that lesson: a router "up" while serving nothing, a judge "stable" while its choices reversed. Measure what your metric can't see, or the metric is the product.
